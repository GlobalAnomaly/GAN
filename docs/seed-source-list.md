# Seed Source List

*The specific archives, databases, and channels the bot starts from. Organized by credibility tier — which classification each feeds, how the bot reaches it, and the access/copyright rule that governs it.*

**How to read this:** work top-down. Tier A is public-domain, official, and highest-credibility — start there and you get hundreds of solid, sourceable cases fast. Expand outward to social and non-English once the pipeline runs. Verify each URL and its reuse terms when you build; sites move and terms change.

---

## Tier A — Official government sources → feeds *Acknowledged*

The gold standard. Officially released material, often public domain, with named authorities and no conventional explanation. This is where your "acknowledged, unexplained" category lives.

**United States — the freshest vein right now.** The Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) is actively releasing declassified UAP tranches under the current "PURSUE" disclosure program, hosted on an official government site. New batches — including never-before-seen military videos and historical files back to the 1940s — have been dropping through 2026. This is the single richest source of current, high-credibility, embeddable content you have; make it the bot's top monitoring priority.
- AARO official publications and reports
- The U.S. National Archives online catalog (Project Blue Book, Roswell films, and tens of thousands of digitized UFO pages — searchable, public domain)
- The FBI's public reading room ("The Vault") and the CIA FOIA reading room
- Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) UAP reports

**United Kingdom.** The National Archives holds the Ministry of Defence UFO files (1950s–2000s), including the official single-sheet record of Rendlesham Forest. Public, catalogued, with contextual research guides.

**France.** GEIPAN, the official UAP unit of the national space agency CNES, runs the most transparent government database in the world — a searchable archive with downloadable investigation files, standardized A/B/C/D classifications, and gendarmerie reports. Doubles as a Tier-D non-English source (see below).

**Historical / cross-national.** The Project Blue Book Archive compiles 18,000+ declassified documents — the USAF's 1947–1969 investigation plus CIA, FBI, NSA, and files from ten-plus countries (British, Brazilian, Swedish, and more), full-text searchable via OCR.

**Expansion targets** (add as you grow): declassified archives from Brazil, Chile's historical CEFAA, Canada, and other national bodies — many surface through the Blue Book Archive above.

**Access & copyright rule for Tier A:** U.S. federal works are public domain — you can cite facts freely and even link/host the documents. For UK, French, and other national archives, facts are free but check each country's reuse/crown-copyright terms before re-hosting a file; linking is always safe. In every case, the recap is written in your own words and the "read the full PDF" button points at the original.

## Tier B — Established research databases → feeds *Unverified* (with structure)

Large, structured catalogs of public sightings. High volume, self-submitted, and mostly unverified — exactly your "unverified" tier, and useful because the data comes pre-fielded (date, place, shape, duration).

**NUFORC (National UFO Reporting Center).** The oldest and largest civilian database — roughly 170,000 public reports, with its own internal tiering (dramatic structured-craft reports down to likely-explainable ones) that helps you triage. It explicitly makes no claim as to any report's validity, which fits your neutral stance perfectly.

**MUFON (Mutual UFO Network).** Fewer cases but field-*investigated*, so higher evidentiary quality. Note: much of the full case detail sits behind paid membership — use what's public, don't circumvent the gate.

**Access & copyright rule for Tier B:** treat these as *leads plus data points*. The factual fields (date, location, shape) aren't copyrightable and are fine to use; the witnesses' narrative text belongs to the source, so write your own account rather than lifting theirs. Be a good citizen technically — pull gently/throttled, don't hammer their servers. Third-party aggregator maps that merge these databases exist, but go to the primary source rather than copying a compilation.

## Tier C — Video & social → feeds *Unverified* (embedded)

Raw footage and eyewitness clips. Embedded, never re-hosted.

**YouTube** — reached through the official Data API for search, metadata, and captions. Mix of reputable UAP-focused channels and raw public uploads. Embeds are the intended use.

**TikTok** — manual only. It's hostile to automation with no friendly content API, so the workflow is: you find the clip, paste the embed link into the admin panel. Don't try to automate it.

**Instagram / X** — manual embed as relevant, same as TikTok.

**Access rule:** embed via each platform's official player; the video bandwidth stays on their servers, not yours. Store the source URL plus the bot's captured description so a dead embed doesn't erase the case.

## Tier D — Non-English sources → your genuine edge

Most English-language UFO sites ignore these, so this is where you can be uniquely comprehensive. The bot transcribes, translates, and *synthesizes* into an English account (per the editorial template).

- **GEIPAN** (French, official — already listed in Tier A)
- Foreign-government archives (Brazilian, Chilean, Swedish, Japanese, etc.)
- Foreign-language YouTube channels and regional news covering local sightings

**Access rule:** same tier rules apply by source type — official archives follow Tier A, social follows Tier C. The added step is transcription + translation before synthesis.

---

## Tier E — News & journalism → context only, *never copied*

Reputable outlets are excellent for learning that a case exists and confirming basic facts (who, when, official response). They are **leads, not content.**

**Access & copyright rule for Tier E — the strict one:** pull the factual skeleton and, at most, one short attributed quote from a *primary* speaker (a pilot, an official). Never reproduce a journalist's paragraph or mirror their article's wording or structure. Relay the witness; don't re-host the writer. This is the rule most likely to get a site in trouble, so it's the one held tightest.

## Seeding sequence (recommended)

1. **Start with Tier A public-domain U.S. sources + GEIPAN + NUFORC data.** This alone gives you hundreds of well-sourced cases across the credibility spectrum, fast, with minimal legal risk.
2. **Prioritize monitoring the U.S. PURSUE/AARO releases** — it's the freshest, highest-value "acknowledged" content and it's actively updating in 2026.
3. **Add YouTube (API) for embedded footage** once the write-and-review flow works end to end.
4. **Layer in non-English and manual TikTok** as the pipeline matures.
5. **Keep news outlets as discovery only**, never as a content source.
